# 0.10.7 claim finalization and removal decisions

Status: released 0.10.7.

## One public-claim finalization helper

Synchronous public control detaches owned provider handles before a registry
transition can remove its declaration. The private finalizer previously sent
successful registry transitions to a second helper with only that one caller.

`finish_detached_claim_transition` now owns both outcomes directly, and
`finish_claim_transition` is deleted. A registry error still restores every
detached handle before returning the original error. Failed restoration still
retires the exact claim and returns the restoration error, subject to cleanup
failure. A successful registry transition still applies its provider effect;
finalized control failures are returned directly, while unexpected provider
failures retire the claim. Borrow release, handle draining and provider ordering
are unchanged.

Callback finalization remains separate. In particular, unexpected Watchdog work
completion failures must trap for IC rollback instead of following synchronous
public control's restoration and retirement rules. Effect confirmation remains
necessary to distinguish requested arms from applied provider operations.

## One ordinary removal decision per transition

Ordinary completion repeated the same declaration-lifetime and pending-unregister
rule in normal stop, explicit invariant failure, directive failure and generation
failure exits. It now computes `remove_on_stop` once from the authorized entry
and pending command. Each terminal exit uses that decision. A successful arm
always retains the entry, including a remove-on-stop declaration that continues.

Successful ordinary cancellation now derives removal from the original execution
state and declaration lifetime rather than assigning a mutable flag in multiple
branches. Running work remains registered while its pending cancellation is
arbitrated. Inactive and immediately cancelled transient declarations are removed.
The checked cancellation-failure exit retains its existing finalization.

This deletes duplicated policy decisions and two mutable removal flags without
adding a shared policy framework. Pending-command precedence, inactive reasons,
cancellation and completion accounting, claim fencing and callback cleanup are
unchanged. These are private, volatile simplifications with no public API,
snapshot, stored-data or generated-artifact changes. They fit a patch release
and require no downstream adapter or compatibility path.

is deleted; the automated contributor has not rerun the lint gate.

Existing restoration-draining, provider-installation and confirmation-failure
fixtures cover the other finalizer exits. Ordinary terminal-failure/lifetime,
pending-command and completion fixtures retain their removal and preservation
checks. No new test suite or private source-layout assertion is added.
