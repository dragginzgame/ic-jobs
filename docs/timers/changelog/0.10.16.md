# 0.10.16 measurement ownership and failure accounting

Status: released 0.10.16.

## Measurement ownership fixture

The existing runtime identity-reuse fixture retains an old callback token after
removing its transient declaration and reusing the identity for a retained Once
registration. It still checks that stale handle consumption preserves the new
registration's armed wakeup.

The extended fixture submits a nonzero instruction/page measurement directly to
the accounting boundary. With no declaration, the write is discarded without
recreating an entry. After identity reuse, it leaves the replacement's empty
performance summaries unchanged. The replacement callback then executes normally
and records seven simulated instructions, Wasm pages from one to three and stable
pages from zero to three. Scheduler summaries remain unchanged. A further stale
measurement leaves all populated performance summaries unchanged as well.

Direct submission exercises the exact-claim guard independently of stale
dispatch rejection, which returns before taking a measurement. It does not prove
that a real expired delivery reaches this boundary or establish IC rollback.
The new registration's normal callback provides the positive recording case;
no alternate test-only accounting implementation is introduced.

## Failure-accounting fixtures

The existing ordinary and Watchdog lifetime matrices retain generation
exhaustion, terminal state, cleanup and removal assertions. Their retained
completion cases now assert the outcome observations alongside the counters.

Ordinary completion turns a failed successor allocation into an invariant
completion while preserving reported work count. Its fixture checks one invariant
completion, no successful completion and the latest invariant outcome.

Watchdog completion records the consumer's success before replacing its committed
successor. Allocation exhaustion stops control with `GenerationExhausted` without
reclassifying that completion. Its fixture checks the latest success, reported
work count and success timestamp, with no failure-completion timestamp. This
preserves the policy distinction exposed by the 0.10.15 assertion correction.
